# U.S. Food Surplus Analysis

## Project overview
This project analyzes published estimates of U.S. food surplus. It looks at the national trend from 2016 to 2024 and compares five sectors for 2024.

## Business questions
- How did estimated U.S. food surplus change from 2016 to 2024?
- Which sectors had the highest and lowest estimated surplus in 2024?

## Tools used
- Python and pandas: loaded and checked the data, summarized values, and explored the annual trend.
- MySQL: queried the data, joined the annual trend to 2024 sector results by year, and calculated sector shares.
- Power BI: built the dashboard.

## Key findings
- The annual estimate peaked at 74.6 million U.S. short tons in 2019, fell to 56.4 million in 2020, and was 70.0 million in 2024.
- Residential was the largest 2024 sector at 23.4 million U.S. short tons.
- Retail was the smallest 2024 sector at 3.98 million U.S. short tons.

## Data and limitations
The annual trend covers 2016–2024, but sector breakdowns are available only for 2024. These are published estimates, not direct measurements from individual businesses or households. “Surplus food” is not the same as food sent to landfill; some surplus may be donated, repurposed, or used as animal feed. The data shows changes over time but does not explain their causes.
